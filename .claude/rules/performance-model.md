# Performance cost facts

Loaded when a session touches an AST-processing module, `Core/OpsConcrete.hs` or anything under `bench/`; a human arrives from the pointer in the root `CLAUDE.md`'s Performance model section, which keeps the rules for *reasoning about* these numbers — criterion-only, slopes not means, attribution by controlled experiment — resident beside that pointer.

File:line citations were verified against the tree at commit `2d16de4e7` (2026-08-14); the citation pass proves a cited line exists — this stamp, that it still says what the claim around it needs.

Cost facts worth knowing before optimizing:

- **The two AD pipelines have different cost shapes.** `cgrad`/`cvjp` re-run differentiation on every call; the symbolic tools (`grad`/`vjp`) pay tracing + `simplifyUserCode` + AD + `simplifyArtifactRev` once — a roughly size-independent overhead — then only interpret the artifact per call. Intended usage: build the artifact once (`vjpArtifact`/`gradArtifact`/`revArtifactAdapt`), interpret it many times (`vjpInterpretArtifact`); a benchmark calling full `vjp` per iteration conflates pipeline cost with execution cost.
- **`sreplicate`, `sreplicateN`, `stranspose` are metadata-only** (stride tricks; ox-arrays `X.replicate` is stretch-to-stride-0), and the ox-arrays dot and elementwise kernels (`Data.Array.Strided.Arith`) are stride-aware. Broadcasts and transposes fed into a dot or a `*` do **not** materialize, so don't rewrite them away on the assumption that they allocate.
- **`tssumN` is iterated `tssum`** (`Core/OpsConcrete.hs:222`): one full pass and allocation per summed dimension.
- **Interpreted `sgather` dominates gather-heavy programs** (the im2col patch arrays of convolution gradients — often ~two thirds of runtime). It runs through ox-arrays' `mgenerate`: per output position (the `shm` dims) evaluate the index and copy a slice (the `shn` dims); the per-element `Int` index arithmetic is the hot loop. One level down, the per-step cost is orthotope's run-decomposition of each strided slice (`toVectorListT`, worth pinning to orthotope-0.1.8.0, the version this repo's plan resolves — though it resolves it from the sibling checkout, which carries that version number plus the fix below — since the name is absent from 0.1.7.2), with a per-element fallback when the innermost dim is strided. That fallback has been replaced in `../orthotope`, which is ahead of the release, not stale: on `speedup-strided-tovector`, at `6ae326e` and to be offered as a PR though none is filed, the via-a-list path becomes a mutable odometer fill through `vFillStrided`, a `Vector` method added for it --- the `mut-odo-vecdims-add-in-leaf-u2` arm, measured on a replica of that path over twenty-odd strategies and thirty-odd shapes at a geomean 0.054 of the old fallback --- and `cabal.project.local` currently carries an active `packages: ../orthotope`, so a build here links the fix, and the figures below are from that build unless marked *released* or *`bq-expand`*, re-measured at `-A32m` on 2026-08-27. Measured against the released fallback (2026-07-31, three interleaved A/B pairs over `gather48`/`scatter48`, the builds differing only in which orthotope they link, per-iteration OLS slope, every fit R² ≥ 0.99, control moving 0.7%), the then-`bq-expand` fix was worth 3.96–5.57× on the two-gather chains and 4.7–7.1× in allocation, and on the conv sweep itself 1.6× at `6x6` rising to 3.5–4.2× from `24x24` up, at 2.1–6.5× less allocation --- the shipped fill halves the two-gather chain's allocation again, 4.60MB against that build's 9.44MB on `gather48`, and has not been A/B'd against the release; the *fused* variants, which never reach the changed code (byte-identical allocation), appeared ~18% slower in that A/B — a position effect of leftover RTS pool state, not the fix: the account is in `docs/position-effect.md`, the pin-the-selection rule in `bench/CLAUDE.md`. Two benchmark-confirmed consequences, each remeasured across both fixes and moving opposite ways under the second: (1) `contractAst` sorts a gather's `shn` dimensions ascending, via metadata-only compensating transposes, so the largest dimension is innermost and the copy's inner loop amortizes the per-step overhead --- a warrant the fill has all but retired, at 1.05x in time and nothing at all in allocation, 4.62MB against 4.60MB per iteration on `gather48`, where `bq-expand` measured 1.65x and a halving, 4.93MB against 9.44MB, and released 1.27x and no halving. What the sort bought was the unsorted orientation's per-step overhead, which the fill no longer pays, so whether the rule still earns its place is open, property 8 passing only because the sort is no loss. A separate fact, and not an alternative the rule could reach for --- `two-gathers-vec-orient` is a different program rather than a rewrite of `two-gathers-ad-orient`, its result dims being ordered differently --- is that the natively well-oriented chain beats the rewritten one, 0.488ms against 0.568ms, the two carrying the same sorted `shn` and 144 positions of 1296 elements either way: that 16% is what the trailing-dim compensating transposes cost, `shm` order being inert by property 7's own control (0.598ms against 0.601ms). Reordering the `shm` dims, by contrast, changes nothing, in time or in allocation, as before; (2) fusing two gathers into one is a *pessimization* --- it trades few large-slice copies for many tiny ones while `mgenerate`'s cost is per position --- costing 20.37x (`bq-expand`: 9.53×; released: 2.25×), the fill having cut the two-gather chain it is measured against while leaving the fused form untouched, so the phase-gating that prevents that fusion is intentional, and sorting cannot rescue the fused form (its `shm` order measures flat, the `fused-gather-shm-sorted-*` benches; its `shn` is tiny and already sorted, fusion having consumed the large dims into the index function). Reducing the remaining cost had two candidate designs and neither is now open work. The upstream one was two-stage: its first stage is the fallback fix above, which has shipped and been measured, leaving only its second, a C strided copy in ox-arrays' cbits ([#123](https://github.com/Mikolaj/horde-ad/issues/123) material). The other, the client-side add-zero gather — gather rebuilt on the scatter model, each strided slice densified through the stride-aware C arith kernels by adding a replicated scalar zero (normalize-in-C with a fused redundant add) — is refuted rather than pending: it needed no upstream release, but the `scatter48` bound that priced it at ~9–12× was measured away by that same fix (scatter bullet below), so do not build it on those numbers.
- **Concrete scatter is slice-based and was markedly cheaper than gather on the same index map and strided-read workload; the fill has closed that gap --- 1.18x against gather's natural orientation and 1.12x against its `shn`-sorted one** (`scatter48` 0.506ms, `bq-expand`: 2.52× and 1.53×), **and gather's fastest orientation now edges past it**, `two-gathers-vec-orient` at 0.488ms; allocation moved the same way, 3.83MB per iteration against gather's 4.60MB where `bq-expand` had 3.87MB against 9.44MB --- which measures what the interpreter builds per position rather than elements moved, so it sharpens the claim rather than contradicting it. Against *released* orthotope the time gap was ~9–12× (`scatter48` ~0.55ms vs `gather48` ~5–7ms), an order of magnitude that was a property of the released fallback and was measured away by its fix (gather bullet above), scatter itself unmoved — so any design priced against that headroom is priced against numbers that are gone. As gather's transpose it performs the same element transfers and, where the index map is many-to-one, even more arithmetic — it sums where gather copies — so the gap is code path, not data volume. The path: `tscatterZSScalar` (`Core/OpsConcrete.hs:1581`) does per-position index eval + `IntMap.insertWith (+)` with whole-slice adds through the stride-aware `NumElt` C kernels, then flat `VS.copy` write-outs — the interpreted Haskell is per *chunk*, all element traffic is C. The accumulation is what dodges the Haskell strided-normalize (it consumes strided views in C and leaves dense buffers to memcpy), a trick the current gather doesn't borrow, though the add-zero design above shows how a sum-free gather could: force a redundant add. Corollaries: extending the `shn`-sort to scatter is an outright pessimization — 3.22x in time on the current build (`two-scatters-ad-shn-sorted`, 1.630ms vs 0.506ms; released, ~4.7×, 2.59ms vs 0.55ms) and 3.26x in allocation (12.50MB vs 3.83MB; `bq-expand`, 14.40MB vs 3.87MB; released, 17.66MB against 3.91MB) --- each fix moves only the sorted variant, its slice views being the only scatter traffic that reaches the fallback, and each slice is added as one flat vector, so a sorted `shn` has no per-`shn`-dim loop to amortize while the compensating transposes leave the slice views strided; scatter in its natural orientation *was* the empirical bound for a fast gather path (its strid-ified and fused forms are far slower) and the fill has reached it, so that headroom is spent rather than pending; and a rewrite's whole-program effect grows with problem size, converging toward the dominant op's win (the `shn`-sort was negligible on small overhead-bound nets and ~17% on a 24x24 CNN gradient on `bq-expand`, a figure its collapse above must have taken down with it, unmeasured since).
- **`vjpArtifact` does not run `contractAst`** — it runs `simplifyUserCode` + `revProduceArtifactDt`; `contractAst`'s only caller is `simplifyInlineContract` (`Core/AstEngine.hs:52-56`, which applies `contractAst` twice), and `simplifyInlineContract` is in turn what `simplifyArtifactRev` and its `Fwd` sibling apply to artifacts — and what convVjpBench's handwritten-term variants call directly, on a term rather than through an artifact. Outside its definition in `Core/AstTraverse.hs`, the name occurs only at that one call site and in `bench/ConvVjpBench.hs` comments. Hence the *raw* artifact is contraction-independent — which explains `S-exec-raw`'s role as an A/B control and means "artifact" benchmarks measure different pipelines depending on whether they stop at `vjpArtifact` or add `simplifyArtifactRev`.

Adding a `contractAst` rewrite rule: put it in `contractAst`, not a smart constructor (which fires in every phase and reshapes intermediates other rules match on), applied at a node's exit and *after* any case that hoists structure out of the same constructor and re-enters `contractAst`. Make it idempotent, and identify which existing rules could undo the form it introduces — for the `shn`-sort, `astTransposeS`/`astGatherKnobsS` only absorb *leading*-dims permutations while the compensating transposes permute trailing dims — recording that argument as a code comment at the rule.
