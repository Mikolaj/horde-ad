# Performance cost facts

Loaded when a session touches an AST-processing module, `Core/OpsConcrete.hs` or anything under `bench/`; a human arrives from the pointer in the root `CLAUDE.md`'s Performance model section, which keeps the rules for *reasoning about* these numbers --- criterion-only, slopes not means, attribution by controlled experiment --- resident beside that pointer.

File:line citations were verified against the tree at commit `2d16de4e7` (2026-08-14); the citation pass proves a cited line exists --- this stamp, that it still says what the claim around it needs.

Cost facts worth knowing before optimizing:

- **The two AD pipelines have different cost shapes.** `cgrad`/`cvjp` re-run differentiation on every call; the symbolic tools (`grad`/`vjp`) pay tracing + `simplifyUserCode` + AD + `simplifyArtifactRev` once --- a roughly size-independent overhead --- then only interpret the artifact per call. Intended usage: build the artifact once (`vjpArtifact`/`gradArtifact`/`revArtifactAdapt`), interpret it many times (`vjpInterpretArtifact`); a benchmark calling full `vjp` per iteration conflates pipeline cost with execution cost.
- **`sreplicate`, `sreplicateN`, `stranspose` are metadata-only** (stride tricks; ox-arrays `X.replicate` is stretch-to-stride-0), and the ox-arrays dot and elementwise kernels (`Data.Array.Strided.Arith`) are stride-aware. Broadcasts and transposes fed into a dot or a `*` do **not** materialize, so don't rewrite them away on the assumption that they allocate.
- **`tssumN` is iterated `tssum`** (`Core/OpsConcrete.hs:222`): one full pass and allocation per summed dimension.
- **Interpreted `sgather` dominates gather-heavy programs** (the im2col patch arrays of convolution gradients --- often ~two thirds of runtime). It runs through ox-arrays' `mgenerate`: per output position (the `shm` dims) evaluate the index and copy a slice (the `shn` dims); the per-element `Int` index arithmetic is the hot loop. One level down, the per-step cost is orthotope's run-decomposition of each strided slice (`toVectorListT`, worth pinning to orthotope-0.1.8.0, the version this repo's plan resolves --- though it resolves it from the sibling checkout, which carries that version number plus the work below --- since the name is absent from 0.1.7.2), which in the release falls back per element when the innermost dim is strided. The checkout is ahead of the release, not stale, and has now replaced that fallback twice: `pr-mikolaj-toVectorListT`, at `3f8bd02` and offered as a PR, canonicalizes a view first (`canonicalizeT` drops size-1 dims and merges adjacent contiguous ones) and dispatches on the canonical form into four regimes --- whole vector, one slice, contiguous runs, strided --- the last two filled through `vFillStrided`, a new `Vector` method carrying an allocate-once mutable odometer fill with zero-stride conditions decided per odometer level; `toVectorT` takes the same dispatch, so the changed code reaches well past the gather path --- though measurably it does not: `shortProdForCI` and `shortMnistForCI`, collected on both orthotopes (2026-08-28), allocate the same to within 0.4% on every one of their 51 benchmarks, geomean 1.000, none of them more. So the FCNN workloads are outside this work's blast radius, and the MNIST suites need no re-collection when it moves again. `cabal.project.local` currently carries an active `packages: ../orthotope`, so a build here links it, and the figures below are that build's unless marked *released*, measured at `-A32m` on 2026-08-28. Measured against released orthotope 0.1.8.0 over the whole suite (2026-08-28, the two builds differing only in which orthotope they link, same recipe and rosters; not interleaved, but allocation is exact and the ratios dwarf the position effect), the shipped dispatch allocates less on every one of the 107 benchmarks --- geomean 1.79x, 8.0--10.1x on the two-gather chains, 2.5--6.7x across the dKrn sweep, 1.6--2.3x on the `cnn-*` groups --- and in time is a geomean 1.55x, 8.0--9.9x on those chains and 1.8--4.4x on the dKrn sweep. The dInp `S-*` variants do not move at all, 1.00--1.03x, which is the control that scatter reaches none of this. Read the time side only where it is large: this is the case the A/B rule in `bench/CLAUDE.md` says pinning the roster does not cover, the two builds moving predecessors' own allocation profiles by up to 10x, so anything inside the position effect's ~10% is undecidable here. Thirty-six benchmarks are slower on the checkout build, all but one under 5% and mostly `H-term` and `S-artifact`, whose allocation did not move at all; the largest is `inp-192x192/H-exec` at +8.7%, the tail of the dInp regression below, allocating 13.3% less while being so. An earlier interleaved A/B (2026-07-31, three pairs over `gather48`/`scatter48`, per-iteration OLS slope, every fit R2 >= 0.99, control moving 0.7%) put an intermediate form of the work at 3.96--5.57x on those chains. In that earlier A/B the *fused* variants appeared ~18% slower at byte-identical allocation --- a position effect of leftover RTS pool state, not the fix: the account is in `docs/position-effect.md`, the pin-the-selection rule in `bench/CLAUDE.md`. That allocation test still classifies correctly, but do not reuse the fused gather as the unaffected control it was then: the canonical dispatch reaches it, taking it from 83.22MB per iteration to 69.79MB and from 12.175ms to 10.801ms (2026-08-28). Two benchmark-confirmed consequences: (1) `contractAst` sorts a gather's `shn` dimensions ascending, via metadata-only compensating transposes, so the largest dimension is innermost and the copy's inner loop amortizes the per-step overhead --- a warrant the checkout's work has mostly retired, at 1.11x in time and nothing in allocation, 4.35MB against 4.40MB per iteration on `gather48`, against 1.27x and no allocation change on *released* orthotope; the 1.65x and the allocation halving that made the strongest case for the rule belonged to an intermediate checkout form and are gone. What the sort bought was the unsorted orientation's per-step overhead, which the dispatch now largely avoids, so whether the rule still earns its place is open, property 8 passing only because the sort is no loss. A separate fact, and not an alternative the rule could reach for --- `two-gathers-vec-orient` is a different program rather than a rewrite of `two-gathers-ad-orient`, its result dims being ordered differently --- is that the natively well-oriented chain beats the rewritten one, 0.445ms against 0.511ms, the two carrying the same sorted `shn` and 144 positions of 1296 elements either way: that 15% is what the trailing-dim compensating transposes cost, steady across both checkout builds measured, `shm` order being inert by property 7's own control. Reordering the `shm` dims, by contrast, changes nothing, in time or in allocation, as before; (2) fusing two gathers into one is a *pessimization* --- it trades few large-slice copies for many tiny ones while `mgenerate`'s cost is per position --- costing 18.98x (released: 2.25x), so the phase-gating that prevents that fusion is intentional, and sorting cannot rescue the fused form (its `shm` order measures flat, the `fused-gather-shm-sorted-*` benches; its `shn` is tiny and already sorted, fusion having consumed the large dims into the index function). Reducing the remaining cost had two candidate designs and neither is now open work. The upstream one was two-stage: its first stage is the fallback work above, which is implemented and measured though still on an unmerged branch, leaving only its second, a C strided copy in ox-arrays' cbits ([#123](https://github.com/Mikolaj/horde-ad/issues/123) material). The other, the client-side add-zero gather --- gather rebuilt on the scatter model, each strided slice densified through the stride-aware C arith kernels by adding a replicated scalar zero (normalize-in-C with a fused redundant add) --- is refuted rather than pending: it needed no upstream release, but the `scatter48` bound that priced it at ~9--12x was measured away by that same fix (scatter bullet below), so do not build it on those numbers.
- **Concrete scatter is slice-based and was markedly cheaper than gather on the same index map and strided-read workload; the checkout has closed that gap --- 1.12x against gather's natural orientation and level with its `shn`-sorted one, 1.01x** (`scatter48` 0.507ms), **while gather's `vec` orientation now runs 1.14x faster than scatter**, 0.445ms against 0.507ms; allocation still favours scatter and by much less than it did, 3.80MB per iteration against gather's 4.40MB --- which measures what the interpreter builds per position rather than elements moved, so it sharpens the claim rather than contradicting it. Against *released* orthotope the time gap was ~9--12x (`scatter48` ~0.55ms vs `gather48` ~5--7ms), an order of magnitude that was a property of the released fallback and was measured away by its replacement (gather bullet above), scatter itself unmoved across every checkout build measured --- so any design priced against that headroom is priced against numbers that are gone. As gather's transpose it performs the same element transfers and, where the index map is many-to-one, even more arithmetic --- it sums where gather copies --- so the gap is code path, not data volume. The path: `tscatterZSScalar` (`Core/OpsConcrete.hs:1581`) does per-position index eval + `IntMap.insertWith (+)` with whole-slice adds through the stride-aware `NumElt` C kernels, then flat `VS.copy` write-outs --- the interpreted Haskell is per *chunk*, all element traffic is C. The accumulation is what dodges the Haskell strided-normalize (it consumes strided views in C and leaves dense buffers to memcpy), a trick the current gather doesn't borrow, though the add-zero design above shows how a sum-free gather could: force a redundant add. Corollaries: extending the `shn`-sort to scatter is an outright pessimization --- 3.21x in time on the current build (`two-scatters-ad-shn-sorted`, 1.629ms vs 0.507ms; released, ~4.7x, 2.59ms vs 0.55ms) and 3.28x in allocation (12.44MB vs 3.80MB; released, 17.66MB against 3.91MB) --- the earlier replacements moved the sorted variant alone, its slice views being the only scatter traffic that reached the fallback, and the canonical dispatch moves neither orientation at all, which is what makes scatter the control it is here; each slice is added as one flat vector, so a sorted `shn` has no per-`shn`-dim loop to amortize while the compensating transposes leave the slice views strided; scatter in its natural orientation *was* the empirical bound for a fast gather path (its strid-ified and fused forms are far slower) and the checkout has passed it, so that headroom is spent rather than pending; and a rewrite's whole-program effect grows with problem size, converging toward the dominant op's win (the `shn`-sort was negligible on small overhead-bound nets and ~17% on a 24x24 CNN gradient on an earlier checkout build, a figure its collapse above must have taken down with it, unmeasured since).
- **`vjpArtifact` does not run `contractAst`** --- it runs `simplifyUserCode` + `revProduceArtifactDt`; `contractAst`'s only caller is `simplifyInlineContract` (`Core/AstEngine.hs:52-56`, which applies `contractAst` twice), and `simplifyInlineContract` is in turn what `simplifyArtifactRev` and its `Fwd` sibling apply to artifacts --- and what convVjpBench's handwritten-term variants call directly, on a term rather than through an artifact. Outside its definition in `Core/AstTraverse.hs`, the name occurs only at that one call site and in `bench/ConvVjpBench.hs` comments. Hence the *raw* artifact is contraction-independent --- which explains `S-exec-raw`'s role as an A/B control and means "artifact" benchmarks measure different pipelines depending on whether they stop at `vjpArtifact` or add `simplifyArtifactRev`.

Adding a `contractAst` rewrite rule: put it in `contractAst`, not a smart constructor (which fires in every phase and reshapes intermediates other rules match on), applied at a node's exit and *after* any case that hoists structure out of the same constructor and re-enters `contractAst`. Make it idempotent, and identify which existing rules could undo the form it introduces --- for the `shn`-sort, `astTransposeS`/`astGatherKnobsS` only absorb *leading*-dims permutations while the compensating transposes permute trailing dims --- recording that argument as a code comment at the rule.
